Bottom line: {{supp}} might reduce migraine days a little, but the evidence is weak and the trials disagree. It is not shown to cut migraines in half.

- The first trial saw a small benefit: "migraine days fell from 6.1 to 4.9 per month with {{supp}}, compared with 6.0 to 5.6 with placebo" (sources/trial-2021-hansen.md). That is less than one migraine day a month.
- A larger and longer trial saw no real difference: "{{supp}} did not reduce monthly migraine days compared with placebo" (sources/trial-2023-okafor.md).
- A review of 3 trials says "{{supp}} may slightly reduce migraine days, but the effect is uncertain" (sources/review-2024.md).
- The WellnessDaily blog claims it cuts migraines in half, but neither trial reports that, and the sentence it puts in the researchers' mouths is not in the 2021 paper (sources/blog-wellnessdaily.md).

For the shop page: say it may help a little for some people, that the studies disagree, and that people with frequent migraines should talk to a doctor.
