Bottom line: you can keep a customer's data for up to 90 days after the contract ends, then you must erase it, except invoices and their records, which you must keep for 5 years for tax.

- The rule: "A trader shall erase the personal data of a consumer no later than 90 days after the end of the contract" (sources/act-section-12.md).
- The exception: the erasure duty "does not apply to records that the trader must keep under tax law" (sources/act-section-12.md), and the tax code says invoices "shall be kept for 5 years from the end of the calendar year in which they were issued" (sources/tax-code-excerpt.md). Use them only for tax.
- The clock starts "when the contract ends" (sources/regulator-faq.md), which can be later than the day someone cancels.
- If a customer asks you to erase their data, you have "within 30 days of the request" (sources/act-section-12.md).
- The law firm alert says everything must go 30 days after cancellation with no exceptions; that is not what the Act says (sources/law-firm-summary.md).
