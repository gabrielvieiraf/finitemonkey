Bottom line: the 9.8% figure is nominal, so it includes price rises. In real terms the economy grew 4.1% in 2025, solid but not a boom, and growth is expected to slow.

- The statistics office: "Gross domestic product at current prices rose 9.8% in 2025. In volume terms, measured at chained 2015 prices, GDP grew 4.1%" (sources/stats-office-release.md).
- The newspaper presents 9.8% at current prices as if it were real growth, and its quote of the statistics office is not in the release (sources/newspaper.md).
- On the upside, the central bank calls 4.1% "strong by historical standards" (sources/central-bank-note.md), but it expects "real growth to slow to 2.5% in 2026" (sources/central-bank-note.md).
- On the downside, an economist notes that "Household spending, the part that matters for shops, grew only 2.1% in real terms" (sources/analyst-blog.md).

For your decision: plan on a market growing a few percent a year in real terms, not ten.
